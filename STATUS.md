## Code state
- **Port and rewrite.** oconnor23 (r22.05.1) was ported to r26.04.1, then rewritten for exact energy
  conservation:
  - E_orb uses the star's actual potential. The heat per step equals E_orb(a_old) − E_orb(a_new).
  - The orbit takes RK4 sub-steps within each MESA step.
  - The deposition kernel is normalised by fractional cell overlap.
  - State lives in `s% xtra/lxtra` (retry- and restart-safe).
  - Tidal heat is deposited in the envelope.
  - Drag coefficients are regular at Mach 1.
  - The first step is limited by the orbit.
  - Physics is split into one module per process, following MESA-Engulf.
- **Defaults match O'Connor+23:** TDC with α_MLT = 2; drag = max(C_d πR_p², C_g πR_a²) ρv²; heat over R_p ± H_P;
  a09 opacities, Zbase = 0.0142; disruption by Roche-lobe overflow or ram pressure. The two r22 defaults that
  changed in r26 (gold2 tolerances, time-centred eps_grav) are pinned to their r22 values.
- **Outflow switch `x_integer_ctrl(4)`:**
  - 0: off.
  - **1: option A (default)**, drag energy removes surface gas instead of heating. `x_integer_ctrl(5)` sets
    the form:
    - **3 = v2, default.** A time-dependent Ivanova & Nandez 2016 rule (Eq. 32). While the drag energy
      released exceeds the binding of the gas outside min(a, R* − R_inf) plus the energy already spent,
      ε_out = `x_ctrl(18)` = 1 of the drag power removes surface gas at its Bernoulli cost.
    - Otherwise the fraction is ε_deep (`x_ctrl(19)` = 0) + ε_wake (`x_ctrl(20)` = **0.25**, calibrated on
      Yang+26) × the gravitational share of the drag.
    - A smooth switch of width `x_ctrl(21)` = 0.2 joins the two regimes. A hard switch crashes the solver.
    - 1 = constant ε (`x_ctrl(15)`); 2 = Γ-limited.
  - 2: option B, which removes outward-moving gas with positive Bernoulli parameter.
- **Other controls.** `x_ctrl(17)` caps dt at a fraction of t_dyn while the companion is in contact (use ~0.1 to
  resolve the hydrodynamic response). History columns `engulf_Mout_*R0` / `engulf_Munb_*R0` give the outward
  mass flux through 1, 2, 4 R0, matching Yang+26 Eqs. 11–12.

## Key findings so far (figures in `docs/figures/`)
1. **Energy conservation** (fig1). The heat the code sets matches MESA's integral to ≲1e-13 per step. MESA's
   own energy error is ≤5e-4 of the injected energy. The orbit ledger closes to ≤5e-4. Checked on six runs.
2. **Bugs in the r22 code** (fig2, full list in PLAN.md Phase 2):
   - The 1.2 Myr first step aged the 4 R☉ star to 4.15 R☉ before the orbit started.
   - dE_orb/da lacked 2πGρa, making the inspiral too fast by up to ~2× in the envelope.
   - The injected-energy counter was never incremented.
   - C_g could be negative.
   - Mesh refinement never ran.
   - Restarts used a stale separation.
3. **Speed** (fig3). Extended hosts are fast: a full 10 R☉ + 1 M_J engulfment takes ~900 models / 20 min,
   agreeing with the old r15140 run. Compact hosts (2–4 R☉) stall with both the old and new code. That stall is
   numerical: Newton divergence in a thin, T-inverted shell at the old photosphere. **Parked.**
4. **Disruption.** The new code stops at Roche-lobe overflow (1.34 R☉ in the 10 R☉ giant). The old code had
   only ram pressure, so the planet went to 0.65 R☉. Which criterion to use is a paper-level choice.
5. **1D hydro cannot eject** (figs 4 and 6):
   - Quasi-static cases (10 R☉ + 1 M_J) unbind nothing, as in O'Connor+23.
   - The AGB200 + 10 M_J case has a supersonic surface (R 203 → 809 R☉, L ×5) but still unbinds nothing.
   - For the Yang+26 setup (1 M☉ / 100 R☉ + 5 M_J), the resolved B run ejects >10× less than 3D and unbinds
     nothing. The orbit agrees: C_g = 1.6–3.1 vs Yang's 1.5–3, and a comparable inspiral time.
   - The diagnosis in the literature (Ivanova & Nandez 2016) is that 3D deposits energy mechanically while 1D
     deposits heat, which is radiated away.
6. **Option A v2** (fig7):
   - The static rule reproduces Lau+25 SPH (4 R☉ + 1 M_J) to within ~2.
   - The time-dependent MESA version is within ×2.2 of the static rule (10 R☉ + 10 M_J: 1.3e-4 vs 2.8e-4 M☉).
   - The rule gives zero ejection for 100 R☉ and AGB hosts, consistent with our option B runs and
     O'Connor+23. Yang+26 (eccentric, gravitational-drag dominated) is the outlier.
   - Gravitational wakes carry energy to the outer layers. With ε_wake = 0.25 the Yang case gives
     1.75e-3 M☉, matching Yang+26's 1.5–2e-3 unbound. This is a single calibration point, and their orbit is
     eccentric.
7. **Literature** (fig5; `docs/ejection_calibration_literature.md`):
   - Dynamical ejecta come from the outer layers and are locally energy-limited against the Bernoulli
     deficit (δE_orb + δE_bind = 0).
   - Deeper, the efficiency is ε ≈ 0.02–0.3 (Ricker & Taam 2012: 0.25).
   - Brown-dwarf post-CE binaries give α = 0.24–0.41.
   - The planet regime has only Lau+25 (SPH, ~1e-5 M☉ unbound) and Yang+26 (~2e-3 M☉).

## Open issues
- **ε calibration.** Option A needs v2 (below). The current e_lift, (1+β²)GM/R − u, overcharges compared with
  the Bernoulli deficit.
- **Orbit ledger with mass loss.** The change of E_orb from mass removed between steps should be folded into
  W_pot. The residual is 4.6e-4 in `yang_Amech`.
- **Compact-host stall** (2–4 R☉): numerical; kernel width, mesh, MLT++ and outer-BC tests are pending.
- **Disruption criterion** for the paper: Roche, ram pressure, or ablation (Lau+26).
- **Drag coefficient.** C_d convention vs Lau+26's calibrated η_pres ≈ 0.46.
- **Mesh refinement** (`x_ctrl(14)`) is implemented but untested.
- **r22 reproduction** of the port (needs SDK 22.6.1) was skipped at the user's choice.

## Next steps
1. **Test the wake calibration.** Done at ε_wake = 0.25 on one point. It needs a second gravitational-drag case
   (e.g. a brown dwarf in an RGB star vs Kramer+20, or MacLeod & Loeb 2020), and a decision on how to treat
   Yang's eccentric orbit.
2. **Fix the near-surface solver stiffness.** It blocks compact hosts and massive companions once heat goes
   into tiny outer masses (4 R☉ runs; 10 R☉ + 10 M_J at a ≈ 9.77 R☉). Candidates:
   - kernel over H_P, or a minimum heated mass;
   - mesh refinement (`x_ctrl(14)`);
   - MLT++;
   - moving the outer boundary to lower τ.
3. **Calibrate the q-scaling** with Ivanova & Nandez 2016, Kramer+20 and Passy+12 (stellar companions), and
   check against Sand+20 / Bronner+24.
4. **Light-curve module:** multi-shell Matsumoto & Metzger 2022, driven by Ṁ(t) and the ejecta energy.
